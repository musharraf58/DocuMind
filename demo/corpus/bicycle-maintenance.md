# Basic Bicycle Maintenance

A few minutes of care each week keeps a bicycle safe, quiet and efficient.
Most routine jobs need only a pump, a rag, chain lubricant and a set of
hex keys.

## Before every ride: the quick check

1. **Tyres.** Squeeze them; they should feel firm. Look for cuts or glass.
2. **Brakes.** Pull each lever. It should stop well before touching the
   handlebar, and the bike shouldn't roll when you push it forward.
3. **Quick releases and thru-axles.** Make sure the wheels are clamped
   tight.
4. **Chain.** It should look lightly oiled, not dry or caked in grime.

## Tyre pressure

The right pressure is printed on the tyre's sidewall as a range, in bar
or psi. Tyres lose air slowly even without a puncture, so check them every
week with a pump that has a gauge. Under-inflated tyres feel slow, wear
quickly and puncture more easily when the tyre is pinched against the rim.
Wider tyres run at lower pressures than narrow road tyres.

## Fixing a flat

Remove the wheel, push the valve up into the tyre, and use tyre levers to
lift one side of the tyre over the rim. Pull out the inner tube, then run
your fingers carefully around the inside of the tyre to find what caused
the puncture; otherwise the new tube will puncture too. Put a little air in
the new tube so it holds its shape, tuck it into the tyre, and push the
tyre back onto the rim by hand. Inflate slowly, checking that the tube
isn't trapped between the tyre and the rim.

## The chain

A clean, lubricated chain shifts smoothly and makes the drivetrain last
longer. Wipe the chain with a rag every week or two. Every few hundred
kilometres, or after a wet ride, apply one drop of lubricant to each roller
while turning the pedals backwards, wait a few minutes, then wipe off the
excess: lubricant belongs inside the rollers, and oil on the outside only
collects dirt.

Chains stretch as they wear. A chain checker tool shows when it is time to
replace one. Replacing a worn chain early is cheap; leaving it too long
wears out the cassette and chainrings as well.

## Brakes

For rim brakes, check that the pads hit the rim squarely and still have
their grooves; replace them when the grooves are gone. For disc brakes,
keep oil and lubricant well away from the rotors and pads, because
contaminated pads lose most of their stopping power. Squealing disc brakes
can often be fixed by cleaning the rotor with isopropyl alcohol.

## Gears

If the chain hesitates or skips when shifting, the cable tension usually
needs a small adjustment. Turning the barrel adjuster where the cable
enters the rear derailleur a quarter turn at a time is often enough. Shift
through all the gears after each adjustment.

## When to see a mechanic

Wheels that wobble, creaking from the bottom bracket, or a fork that knocks
when you brake are jobs for a bike shop. So is anything to do with
hydraulic brake lines. A yearly service catches worn parts before they
fail.
