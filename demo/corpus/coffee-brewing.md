# Brewing Better Coffee at Home

Good coffee at home depends on four things you control: the beans, the
grind, the ratio of coffee to water, and the water itself. Expensive
equipment matters far less than getting these right.

## Beans

Coffee tastes best within a few weeks of roasting. Buy whole beans in
small amounts, look for a roast date on the bag, and store them in an
airtight container away from light and heat. The freezer is fine for
long-term storage if the beans are sealed and thawed only once.

Light roasts keep more of the bean's origin flavours: fruit, flowers,
acidity. Dark roasts taste more of the roast itself: chocolate, smoke,
bitterness. Neither is better; they suit different tastes and methods.

## Grind size

Grind just before brewing, because ground coffee goes stale within minutes
to hours. The grind size controls how fast water extracts flavour:

- Coarse, like sea salt, for French press and cold brew.
- Medium, like sand, for drip machines and pour-over.
- Fine, like table salt, for moka pots.
- Very fine, almost powder, for espresso.

A burr grinder gives even particles; a blade grinder makes a mix of dust
and chunks that extracts unevenly. If a cup tastes sour and thin, grind
finer. If it tastes bitter and harsh, grind coarser.

## Ratio

A good starting point is 1 gram of coffee for every 16 grams of water, so
about 60 grams of coffee per litre. Weigh both with a kitchen scale; scoops
vary a lot. Adjust in small steps: more coffee for a stronger cup, less for
a lighter one.

## Water

Coffee is almost all water, so water that tastes bad makes bad coffee.
Filtered water is a simple improvement. The ideal brewing temperature is
between 90 and 96 degrees Celsius, just off the boil: let boiling water
rest for about thirty seconds.

## Methods

**French press.** Add coarse coffee, pour in hot water, stir, and wait four
minutes. Press the plunger slowly and pour at once, so the coffee doesn't
keep extracting. The result is full-bodied with some sediment.

**Pour-over.** Rinse the paper filter with hot water first. Add medium
coffee, pour a little water to wet it all and wait thirty seconds while it
releases gas (the "bloom"), then pour the rest in slow circles. Total time
is about three minutes. The result is clean and bright.

**Cold brew.** Steep coarse coffee in cold water, about 1 part coffee to 8
parts water, for 12 to 18 hours in the fridge, then filter. It makes a
smooth, low-acid concentrate to dilute with water or milk.

## Cleaning

Old coffee oils turn rancid and taint every new cup. Rinse equipment after
each use and wash it with soap regularly. Descale kettles and machines
every few months if your water is hard.
