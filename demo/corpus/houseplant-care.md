# Keeping Houseplants Alive

Most houseplants die from the same few causes: too much water, too little
light, or the wrong pot. Understanding what a plant needs prevents nearly
all of them.

## Light

Light is a plant's food source, and indoor light is much weaker than it
looks to our eyes. A spot a few metres from a window can receive less than
a tenth of the light right next to it.

- **Bright direct light** (a south-facing window in the northern
  hemisphere): cacti, succulents, citrus.
- **Bright indirect light** (near a window, out of the sun's direct rays):
  most tropical plants, such as monstera, fiddle-leaf fig and pothos.
- **Low light** (far from windows): snake plant, ZZ plant, cast-iron plant.
  "Tolerates low light" means survives, not thrives: growth will be slow.

Signs of too little light are long, stretched stems, small new leaves and
leaning towards the window. Rotate plants every few weeks so they grow
evenly.

## Watering

Overwatering kills more houseplants than underwatering. Roots need air as
well as water; soil that stays soaked suffocates them and invites root rot.

Water when the top few centimetres of soil are dry, not on a fixed
schedule, and water thoroughly until it runs out of the drainage holes.
Then let the pot drain completely; never leave it standing in water.
Plants need less water in winter, when growth slows.

Drooping can mean either too much or too little water, so check the soil:
dry and light means thirsty, wet and heavy means overwatered. Yellow lower
leaves with wet soil usually point to overwatering.

## Pots and soil

Always use a pot with drainage holes. A decorative pot without holes can
hold a plastic nursery pot inside it.

Use a potting mix suited to the plant: a gritty, fast-draining mix for
succulents and cacti, and a chunkier mix with bark for plants like orchids
and monstera. Garden soil is too dense for pots.

Repot when roots circle the bottom of the pot or grow out of the drainage
holes, usually every one to two years, and only into a pot a few
centimetres wider. Spring, when growth resumes, is the best time.

## Humidity and temperature

Many tropical plants prefer more humidity than heated homes provide.
Grouping plants together, or placing them on a tray of wet pebbles, raises
the humidity around them slightly. Keep plants away from radiators and
cold draughts; sudden temperature changes cause leaf drop.

## Feeding

Plants growing actively benefit from a diluted liquid fertiliser every few
weeks in spring and summer. Don't fertilise in winter, or a plant that has
just been repotted, since fresh potting mix contains nutrients.

## Pests

Check the undersides of leaves regularly. Spider mites leave fine webbing,
mealybugs look like small tufts of cotton, and fungus gnats are tiny flies
that breed in wet soil. Isolate an affected plant, wipe the leaves, and let
the soil dry out more between waterings to get rid of gnats.
