Larkspur Home: Customer Support Handbook

Larkspur Home is a fictional company invented for the DocuMind retrieval evaluation. Every name, number and rule in this document is made up. This handbook is written as one continuous text on purpose, without headings, so that the evaluation can see how different chunk sizes split a long passage.

Welcome to the support team. Most of what you need to know is in the customer-facing policies on returns, warranty, shipping and the Lantern loyalty programme, and you should read those first, because customers quote them back at us and expect us to know them better than they do. This handbook covers the things customers never see: how we talk to people, what you are allowed to decide on your own, when you must hand a case to someone else, and how we keep our records. Read it in your first week and come back to it whenever something feels unclear. If the handbook and a team lead disagree, the team lead wins for that case, and you should tell the support operations group so the handbook can be fixed.

Our support chat is open from 08:00 to 20:00 on working days and from 10:00 to 16:00 on Saturdays. Email is answered seven days a week, but on Sundays only urgent cases are picked up, and everything else waits for Monday morning. The target is a first reply within four working hours for chat transcripts that were left unanswered and within one working day for email. These targets are measured per team every week and shared on the dashboard in the support room. Nobody is judged on their personal reply times alone; a fast reply that does not solve anything only creates a second contact, and the second contact is what we really try to avoid.

The tone we use is friendly, plain and direct. Write the way you would explain something to a neighbour: short sentences, no jargon, no copied paragraphs from the policies unless the customer asks for the exact wording. Use the customer's name once at the start and do not overdo apologies. One sincere apology for a real mistake is worth more than a sorry in every sentence. Never promise something you cannot check yourself, such as a delivery date the courier has not confirmed. If you do not know the answer, say that you will find out and when you will come back, and then do come back at that time even if you still have no answer.

You are trusted to make many decisions without asking. Agents may approve a goodwill refund of up to 75 euros without asking a team lead, for example when a delivery was late, a part was missing, or the customer had to contact us more than twice about the same problem. Above 75 euros, add a note to the case and assign it to the team lead on duty, who will normally decide the same day. Customers who contact us about a late order more than ten working days after dispatch automatically receive a 15 euro goodwill credit on their account, which you do not need to approve, but you should mention it so the customer knows it is there. Goodwill is always given as a refund to the original payment method or as account credit, never as cash or as a gift card, because gift cards cannot be taken back if the case is later found to be fraudulent.

Some cases are never yours to solve alone. A complaint that mentions fire, smoke, melting, a burning smell or an electric shock is escalated to the product safety team within one hour, whatever time it arrives, and the product is collected from the customer free of charge. Tell the customer to stop using the product and unplug it, do not ask them to send photos of the inside of the product, and do not discuss the cause, even if you think you know it. The safety team contacts the customer directly. Cases involving a journalist, a lawyer or a public complaint on social media with more than a thousand followers go to the communications team. Requests to delete personal data, or to see the personal data we hold, go to the privacy desk, which must answer within thirty days by law, so forward them on the same day you receive them.

Fraud is rare but expensive. Be careful when someone asks to change the delivery address after an order has been dispatched, when the same customer reports several parcels lost within a few months, or when a return arrives containing a different, older product than the one we sent. Do not accuse anyone; mark the case with the fraud flag, write down what made you suspicious, and continue to answer politely. The fraud team reviews flagged cases every morning and will tell you how to proceed. A flag is not a judgement about the customer, and many flagged cases turn out to be honest mistakes.

Every contact must be recorded in the case system, including chats that ended without a solution. Write the case summary for a colleague who has never seen the case: what the customer wanted, what you checked, what you decided and why, and what happens next. Do not write opinions about the customer's character. Customers can request a copy of everything we store about them, including these summaries, and they occasionally do. Close a case only when the customer's problem is solved or when they have not replied for seven days after your last message; in the second situation, send a short closing message that explains how to reopen it.

Finally, look after yourself. Support work is repetitive and sometimes customers are angry for reasons that have nothing to do with you. You may end a chat or a call politely when a customer becomes abusive, after one warning, and you do not need anybody's permission to do so. Take your breaks away from the screen, and talk to your team lead if a case keeps bothering you after work. We would rather lose a difficult customer than a good colleague.
